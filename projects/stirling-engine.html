Stirling Engine
Machined From Raw Stock

Manufacturing Methods & Materials · Southern Alberta Institute of Technology

Overview

This project required designing and machining a fully functional, dynamically balanced Stirling engine entirely from raw stock material — bar stock, plate, and round — using manual lathes and milling machines. Every component was dimensioned, drawn, machined, and assembled by hand, with no pre-made sub-assemblies.

The Stirling engine is a closed-cycle regenerative heat engine that converts a temperature differential into mechanical work. Its external combustion and tight tolerances make it an ideal vehicle for learning precision machining, tolerancing, and assembly fit.

What Was Built

  • Complete Stirling engine assembly: power piston, displacer piston, cylinder, crankshaft, connecting rods, flywheel, and frame
  • All components machined to drawing tolerances on manual lathe and Bridgeport-style vertical mill
  • Flywheel dynamically balanced to minimize vibration at operating speed
  • Final assembly verified functional — engine runs on a temperature differential applied to the hot cap

Manufacturing Process

The project began with engineering drawings produced to ASME standards, establishing fit and tolerance classes for mating surfaces — particularly the piston-to-cylinder clearance, which is critical for compression sealing without excessive friction.

  • Turned cylindrical components (pistons, crankpins, journals) on a manual engine lathe to ±0.001" tolerance
  • Milled flat surfaces, slots, and drilled bolt patterns on a vertical knee mill
  • Used dial indicators and micrometers throughout to verify dimensions at each operation
  • Applied surface finish control — chosen cutting speeds and feeds to achieve required Ra on sealing surfaces
  • Performed dynamic balance of the flywheel by trial weighting on a balance stand

Skills Developed

  • Manual lathe operation: turning, facing, boring, knurling, parting
  • Vertical mill operation: face milling, shoulder milling, slot drilling, precision layout
  • Engineering drawing interpretation and GD&T application in shop context
  • Metrology: micrometer, dial indicator, vernier caliper, surface plate & height gauge
  • Fit selection and tolerancing for moving assemblies

Tools & Equipment
