Combustion Chamber Design
Turbocharger Gas Turbine

Aerospace Propulsion Capstone · Supervised by Dr. Sepehr Mosadegh

Overview

This capstone project aimed to develop a low-cost, small-scale gas turbine as a platform for UAV research and engineering education. The core challenge was designing a compact combustion chamber around a commercial automotive turbocharger — adapting industrial turbomachinery principles to a constrained budget and physical envelope.

The result was a 16.4-inch modular combustion chamber fabricated from low carbon steel, with verified ignition and flame stabilization systems. The project established a proof-of-concept platform where students can directly observe thermodynamics and combustion in operation.

My Contributions

  • Led combustor dimensional development including swirler design, flow designation, and diameter definition
  • Performed analytical process dimensioning in MATLAB to model the combustion process and size critical components
  • Produced 3D geometry in SolidWorks and iterated through design revisions to resolve manufacturing constraints
  • Implemented the fuel supply system and developed associated safety procedures
  • Served as informal team lead for schedule management and quality standards within the 6-member group
  • Conducted design reviews throughout the project lifecycle

Testing & Results

An ambient pressure flame test validated both the ignition system and the liner design's flow characteristics. The test confirmed stable combustion and effective flame stabilization through the swirler geometry. Full compression testing with the turbocharger did not achieve the intended pressure ratios in the final test campaign, but the combustion subsystem itself performed as designed — establishing a solid foundation for future development iterations.

Key Challenges

  • Balancing thermal efficiency targets with the physical constraints of a commercial turbocharger housing
  • Designing a swirler that provided adequate recirculation for flame stabilization at low pressure ratios
  • Coordinating fabrication decisions across a multi-member team to maintain design intent
  • Developing safe fuel handling procedures for an open-air flame test environment
