Materials Selection & Design
High-Temperature Turbine Blade

Engineering Materials · University of British Columbia Okanagan

Overview

This project applied systematic materials selection methodology to one of the most demanding components in mechanical engineering: a high temperature, high pressure turbine blade operating in a gas turbine hot section. The objective was to evaluate candidate materials against a structured set of performance criteria and identify the optimal selection, balancing capability, geometry, and cost.

Turbine blades in the hot section of a gas turbine experience extreme thermal gradients, centrifugal stresses, oxidation, and fatigue — making materials selection a multi-objective engineering problem where no single material excels on all criteria.

Selection Criteria & Approach

  • Identified dominant failure modes: creep, high-cycle fatigue, oxidation, and thermal shock
  • Established performance index expressions from stress and thermal analysis of the blade geometry
  • Evaluated candidate superalloy families (nickel-base, cobalt-base, ceramic matrix composites)
  • Applied cost weighting alongside performance to assess real-world viability
  • Used Ashby-style material property charts to compare candidates across multiple property axes simultaneously
  • Recommended final material selection with justification against all evaluation criteria

Engineering Analysis

Creep resistance at operating temperature was the primary discriminator, as blade tip temperatures in modern gas turbines can exceed the melting points of conventional alloys — requiring directionally solidified or single-crystal superalloys to eliminate grain boundary creep paths. The analysis quantified the trade-off between maximum use temperature, density (affecting centrifugal stress), and material cost per kilogram.

Geometric factors including blade chord, aspect ratio, and cooling channel constraints were incorporated to ensure the selected material was manufacturable in the required form.

Key Takeaways

  • Nickel-base single-crystal superalloys dominate high-pressure turbine blades due to their creep resistance at temperature
  • Thermal barrier coatings (TBCs) effectively extend the operational temperature range beyond bulk material limits
  • Cost and supply chain considerations often constrain materials selection toward second-best technical options
  • Structured multi-criteria methodology prevents bias toward familiar materials when better options exist

Tools & Methods

  • Ashby materials selection charts
  • Creep & fatigue life analysis
  • Multi-criteria decision matrix
  • Performance index derivation
  • Technical report writing
