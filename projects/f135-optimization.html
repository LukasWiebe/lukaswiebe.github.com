F135 Thrust & Fuel Consumption
Optimization

Aircraft Propulsion · University of British Columbia Okanagan

Overview

This project modeled the thermodynamic cycle of the F135 — the low bypass turbofan engine powering the F-35 Lightning II — to systematically optimize its performance parameters. The analysis focused on identifying optimal combinations of bypass ratio and pressure ratio that minimize specific fuel consumption (SFC) and thrust specific fuel consumption (TSFC) while maintaining thrust output.

The work combined classical Brayton cycle analysis with iterative MATLAB computation, producing a parametric study of engine performance across a realistic design space.

Methodology

  • Modeled the ideal thermodynamic cycle of a low bypass turbofan with inlet and efficiency conditions
  • Defined inlet conditions, component efficiencies, and fuel properties consistent with the F135 operating envelope
  • Implemented an iterative MATLAB solver to sweep bypass ratio and pressure ratio across a defined parameter space
  • Generated 3D surface plots mapping SFC and TSFC across the full parameter grid
  • Identified optimal operating points and analyzed trade-offs between thrust density and fuel efficiency

Results & Insights

The 3D optimization graphs revealed clear performance ridges — combinations of bypass and pressure ratio that simultaneously minimize fuel consumption and maximize thrust. The analysis confirmed that the F135's relatively low bypass ratio is a deliberate trade-off favouring thrust density and supersonic performance over cruise fuel efficiency, consistent with its combat aircraft mission profile.

The iterative approach allowed rapid evaluation of hundreds of design points that would be infeasible to assess analytically, demonstrating the value of computational methods in early-stage propulsion design.
